Permanent residency after study works differently in each country: Canada runs a points system (Express Entry) where a Canadian degree plus work experience scores heavily; Australia uses a similar points test; the UK requires a sponsoring job first, with settlement after 5 years; Germany offers PR after roughly 2–4 years of qualified work for graduates.

## Study is the easy half — plan the second half early

Most students choose a country for its universities and only later discover that post-study settlement rules vary enormously. The honest summary: **Canada and Australia have designed their systems to convert students into permanent residents; the UK and Germany expect you to convert into a skilled worker first.** Neither is worse, but they demand different plans. ## Country-by-country comparison

| Route | Typical timeline from graduation | Key requirement | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **UK** — Skilled Worker → settlement | ~5–6 years of continuous work | Job offer from a licensed sponsor meeting salary threshold | Sponsorship gaps break continuity; rules change often |

## United Kingdom: work first, settle later

International graduates cannot apply for PR directly after a degree. The path runs: **Student visa → Graduate Route (2 years, or 3 for PhDs) → Skilled Worker visa → settlement after a qualifying period of continuous residence.**

What actually matters:

- The Graduate Route gives you time to find a sponsoring employer, but those years generally **do not count** toward settlement the way work-visa years do — check the current qualifying rules on gov.uk.
- The Skilled Worker visa requires a licensed sponsor, a qualifying job and a salary threshold. Doctoral-level roles in shortage contexts have different thresholds — the details shift, so verify.
- Once on a work visa, absence from the UK during the qualifying period is capped.

The doctoral advantage: PhD graduates get the longest Graduate Route and typically qualify for roles meeting salary thresholds sooner. ## Canada: the designed-for-PR system

Canada's Express Entry openly favours people who studied and worked there:

1. **Complete a qualifying programme** (8+ months) → get a Post-Graduation Work Permit (up to 3 years).
2. **Work at least 12 months** in Canada (skilled, any employer) → qualify for Canadian Experience Class.
3. **Enter the Express Entry pool** — points for age, education, Canadian experience, and strong English/French. French ability is currently a significant points bonus.
4. **Receive an Invitation to Apply** when a draw matches your score → submit the PR application.

Category-based draws now prioritise specific fields (healthcare, STEM, trades and others), so your programme choice affects your odds. A Master's or PhD plus one year of Canadian work typically produces a competitive score.

## Australia: points and occupation lists

Australia's skilled migration mirrors Canada's logic with different machinery:

- **Points test:** age under 33 scores best, strong English (superior level earns more), Australian study adds points, regional study adds more.
- **Occupation relevance:** your nominating occupation must sit on the relevant list and match your skills assessment.
- **Employer-sponsored visas** (like the 186) offer a parallel route if an employer commits to you.
- **State nomination (190)** can add the points needed when an independent score falls short.

Australian PhD graduates fare well: doctoral study earns solid points and the Temporary Graduate visa (485) provides 2–3 years of open work rights to accumulate experience.

## Germany: fast PR, one condition

Germany is the underrated option. Graduates of German universities can get a residence permit to seek work, then:

- **Settlement (permanent residence) after just 2 years** of qualified employment for German degree holders — 4 years otherwise — provided you have paid into the pension system
- **Basic German (typically B1)** is required for settlement — this is the filter most graduates fail to plan for
- PhD holders move fastest, with EU Blue Card rules offering some of Europe's quickest settlement timelines

Add tuition-free study (see [studying in Germany for free](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/study-in-germany-for-free-complete-guide-2027) and the [blocked account visa requirement](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/blocked-account-germany-student-visa-guide)) and the total cost-to-PR ratio is the best of the four countries.

## How to plan your route in three moves

1. **Pick the country whose PR logic suits your profile** — young with strong English? Canada/Australia points favour you. Research career with a PhD? UK or Germany.
2. **Budget for language early.** German B1 and Australian superior English are both multi-year projects if left late.
3. **Protect continuous residence and qualifying employment.** Gaps, long absences and non-sponsoring employers are what actually sink PR applications.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I get PR in the UK directly after my Master's?**
No. You must first move onto a work visa with a sponsoring employer; settlement follows a qualifying period of continuous residence. The Graduate Route gives you time to find that employer but does not by itself lead to settlement.

**Which country is easiest for PR after study?**
For most profiles, Canada — its Express Entry system is explicitly built to convert international graduates with local work experience. Germany is fastest once you have a job and German B1.

**Does a PhD help with permanent residency?**
Yes, everywhere. Higher education adds points in Canada and Australia, unlocks the 3-year Graduate Route in the UK, and speeds settlement in Germany. PhD graduates also clear salary thresholds sooner.

**Do years on post-study work visas count toward PR?**
In Canada and Australia, work during post-study permits counts directly toward PR eligibility. In the UK, Graduate Route years generally do not count the same way — verify current rules before relying on them.

## Official source

Immigration rules change frequently — all four countries have amended post-study and settlement rules within the last few years. **Verify current requirements on gov.uk, IRCC (Canada), Home Affairs (Australia) and the German residence regulations before planning your application.**
