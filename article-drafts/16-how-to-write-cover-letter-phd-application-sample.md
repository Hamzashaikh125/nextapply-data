**Direct answer box (first 50 words):**

A PhD cover letter is a one-page letter that connects your specific experience to a specific advertised project. Structure it in four moves: why this project, what you bring (evidence), why this group and supervisor, and a closing line about availability. Keep it under 400 words and tailor every paragraph.

---

## When you need a cover letter vs a motivation letter

The terms overlap, and requirements differ by country. As a rule:

| Document | Typical use | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Cover letter | Advertised, funded PhD positions (UK, Netherlands, Nordics, Germany) | 1 page |
| Motivation letter | University-admission PhD and Master's applications (much of continental Europe) | 1–2 pages |

If the advert says "cover letter", keep it tight and job-focused. If it says "motivation letter", you can go slightly longer and more personal — our [motivation letter guide for European universities](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/how-to-write-motivation-letter-european-universities-sample) covers that variant.

## The four-paragraph structure that works

**Paragraph 1 — the project and you, in two sentences.** Name the position exactly as advertised, state when you complete (or completed) your current degree, and give one sentence on why this project is the right next step. No "I am writing to apply for the position of..." padding — the subject line already said that.

**Paragraph 2 — evidence you can do it.** Two or three concrete claims with specifics: a dissertation, a project, a method you have used, a result you produced. Match these to the advert's requirements. This paragraph carries 60% of the letter's weight.

**Paragraph 3 — why this group.** Show you have read their work: name a paper, a method or a strand of the supervisor's research and say — briefly and honestly — how your interests connect. Generic praise ("your university is world-leading") is worse than nothing.

**Paragraph 4 — practical close.** Availability, willingness to interview, and a thank-you. One or two sentences.

## Sample PhD cover letter

> Dear Dr Mensah,
>
> I am applying for the PhD position "Machine learning for early flood warning in data-sparse regions" (ref. PhD-2026-114). I complete my MSc in Hydroinformatics at TU Delft in June 2027, and this project sits exactly where my Master's research and my field experience meet: I want to build forecasting methods that work where ground data does not exist.
>
> My Master's thesis developed a LSTM-based rainfall-runoff model for the Volta basin, achieving a Kling-Gupta efficiency of 0.81 against gauge data — work that required exactly the sparse-data handling this project describes. Before that, I spent two years as a junior hydrologist at the Ghana Water Resources Commission, where I built the data pipeline for 40 river gauges and saw first-hand why station-based forecasting fails across much of the basin. I have worked daily in Python (PyTorch, GeoPandas) and routinely with remote-sensing products such as ERA5 and Sentinel-1.
>
> Your 2024 paper on transfer learning for ungauged catchments shaped how I framed my thesis, and I would be excited to extend that line of work to operational early-warning systems under your supervision. The prospect of combining the hydrology group's modelling expertise with the field partnerships described on the project page is precisely the environment I looked for.
>
> I am available to start in September 2027 and happy to interview at any time, in person or online. Thank you for considering my application.
>
> Yours sincerely,
> Kwame Asante

Notice what that letter does: every claim in paragraph 2 answers something the advert asked for, and paragraph 3 proves genuine reading. That is the whole game.

## Tailoring: the 20-minute method

You cannot send the same letter everywhere and expect interviews. For each application:

1. Copy your master letter into a fresh file.
2. Re-read the advert and underline required skills — our guide to [reading a PhD job advert before applying](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/how-to-read-phd-job-advertisement-before-applying) gives you a checklist for this.
3. Rewrite paragraph 2 so each underlined requirement is answered by one of your examples. Reorder if needed.
4. Rewrite paragraph 3 from scratch. Skim the supervisor's two most recent papers; cite one accurately.
5. Check the project reference number, supervisor name and start date. Wrong-name errors are astonishingly common and fatal.

If you are still at the pre-application stage and writing to a potential supervisor cold, use a shorter email instead — our [supervisor email templates](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/how-to-email-phd-supervisor-uk-7-templates) cover that separately.

## Mistakes that get letters skimmed and dropped

- **Restating the CV.** The letter should interpret, not repeat. If a sentence could appear in either document unchanged, cut it.
- **Praising the university instead of the project.** Panels know their ranking. They want to know you read *their* project.
- **Overclaiming.** Do not call a coursework essay "groundbreaking research". Supervisors detect inflation instantly and discount everything else you wrote.
- **Anything over one page.** If you need a second page, you have not edited hard enough.
- **Missing the actual requirements.** If the advert asks for fieldwork experience and you have some, it must appear in the letter, not just the CV.

## Attaching and sending

PDF only, named `Asante-CoverLetter-PhD114.pdf`. Match the file naming of your CV and, if the advert is a formal application, upload documents in the order listed. If you are emailing the application directly, the letter is the email body *and* the attachment — keep the email itself to three lines.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should a PhD cover letter be?**
One page, roughly 300–400 words. Funded positions attract hundreds of applicants; nobody reads page two.

**Should I address it to the supervisor or "Dear Sir/Madam"?**
Always a named person if one exists in the advert — and there almost always is. "Dear Hiring Committee" only as a last resort.

**Do I need a cover letter if the portal doesn't require one?**
If the field is optional, still include one. It is the only document where your voice addresses the panel directly, and optional fields are where committed applicants differentiate.

**Can I use the same letter for multiple universities?**
The skeleton, yes. Paragraphs 1–3 must be rewritten per application, or the letter will read as a mass-mailout — panels compare notes more often than you think.
