**Direct answer box (first 50 words):**

Yes. International PhD students on the UK Student visa can work up to 20 hours per week during term time and full-time during approved vacations. The rules also allow work placements if listed on your CAS. After completing your PhD, the Graduate Route gives you three years of unrestricted work rights.

---

## The 20-hour rule, precisely

The visa condition reads "up to 20 hours per week during term time." Three details matter:

| Detail | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| Per week, not averaged | 25 hours one week and 15 the next is a breach, even if the average is 20 |
| Term time vs vacation | Full-time work is allowed during officially designated vacation periods; check your university's published dates |
| Counts unpaid work too | Voluntary roles and unpaid internships count toward the limit |
| Placement work | Allowed if the placement is an assessed part of your programme and stated on your CAS |

For doctoral researchers the boundary between "term" and "vacation" is blurrier than for taught students — many universities treat the whole year as term time for PhD candidates, or leave it to your supervisor's agreement. Ask your department what they record officially, because your visa compliance depends on their definition.

## What kind of work is allowed — and what is not

**Allowed:**

- Part-time employment anywhere (campus jobs are common but not required)
- Teaching, demonstrating or marking for your own university
- Freelance or self-employed work, provided it fits within the hours limit — though some universities discourage it for funded students
- Paid research assistant work, including on projects outside your university

**Not allowed:**

- Permanent full-time salaried positions during your studies
- Positions that require a professional practising status you do not hold
- Working as a doctor or dentist in training, or as a professional sportsperson or entertainer
- Exceeding the 20 hours — even unpaid

One nuance worth knowing: if your university employs you on a standard teaching contract, those hours count toward your 20. Demonstrating one undergraduate lab course per week typically eats 4–6 of them.

## Does a stipend count as income?

A studentship stipend is funding, not employment income — it comes with no hours restriction and no tax, but it also gives no National Insurance number route or employment rights. Most funded PhD students in the UK live on the stipend (check how it compares in our [PhD stipend comparison by country](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/phd-salary-stipend-comparison-by-country-2027)) and use permitted part-time work as a buffer rather than a lifeline. ## Practical reality: can you actually do both?

The honest answer: yes, but carefully. A PhD is a full-time job by itself. Students who manage paid work successfully tend to:

- Cap work at 10–15 hours even though 20 is allowed
- Choose flexible, low-cognitive-load jobs (library, hospitality shifts) rather than demanding ones that compete with research
- Cluster work in writing-up years, when supervision is lighter, rather than in year one
- Confirm with their supervisor that outside work will not affect progress — for sponsored or scholarship students, some funders require it

## After the PhD: the Graduate Route

Once you submit (or complete) your doctoral programme, the **Graduate Route** grants three years of unrestricted UK work — no sponsor, no salary threshold, no hour limits. That is the real prize: your part-time job during the PhD matters far less than converting the doctorate into a sponsored role or career during those three years. ## Frequently asked questions

**Can PhD students work full-time in the UK?**
Only during officially designated vacations, if your university defines them for doctoral students — many do not. During term time the limit is 20 hours per week. After completion, the Graduate Route removes all limits for three years.

**Does teaching count toward the 20 hours?**
Yes. Demonstrating, marking and teaching hours are work like any other and must fit within the weekly limit.

**Can I do freelance work on a UK student visa?**
Yes, within the 20-hour weekly cap and alongside your studies — but check your university's policy first, and remember unpaid work counts toward the hours too.

**What happens if I exceed 20 hours?**
It is a breach of visa conditions with serious consequences, including potential visa curtailment and problems with future UK applications. Universities monitor this and report breaches.

## Official source

Work conditions can change with immigration rule updates. **Verify current conditions on gov.uk (Student visa — work conditions) and with your university's international student office before accepting any job.**
