**Direct answer box (first 50 words):**

Send exactly one follow-up, 10–14 working days after your first email, in the same thread, adding new value (a revised idea, a question about their latest paper). If there is still no reply after another two weeks, move on to other supervisors — silence usually means full, away, or unmonitored, not rejected.

## First, why supervisors go quiet

Before assuming the worst, know the common reasons an academic doesn't reply:

- **Admissions-season overload.** Between October and March, popular UK supervisors can receive dozens of enquiries a week. Yours may simply be buried.
- **The project is already spoken for.** Many won't reply once a studentship has an internal frontrunner.
- **Annual leave or fieldwork.** Academics travel for conferences and research for weeks at a time; university auto-replies are unreliable.
- **No funded place to offer.** Some supervisors only reply when they have funding attached, which is poor etiquette but common.
- **A generic first email.** If your original message looked mass-sent, a busy supervisor may have archived it without guilt.

This is why your first email matters — our [guide to emailing a PhD supervisor with 7 templates](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/how-to-email-phd-supervisor-uk-7-templates) is designed to avoid the archive folder in the first place.

## When to follow up

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| 0–10 working days since your email | Wait. Do nothing. |
| 10–14 working days, no reply | Send one short follow-up in the same thread |
| 2 weeks after follow-up, still nothing | Move on; try a second supervisor in the same group |
| Supervisor replied "busy, back in [month]" | Wait until that month, then follow up once |
| Advert deadline is under 3 weeks away | Follow up sooner, and note the deadline politely |

## The follow-up email template

Keep it to four sentences. Same thread, same recipients, no guilt-tripping.

> **Subject:** Re: PhD enquiry — [topic], October 2027
>
> Dear Dr [Surname],
>
> I am following up on my email of [date] about the [project title] PhD. Since writing, I have [one new thing: read your paper on X / drafted a one-page proposal outline / confirmed my [test/qualification] result] — attached in case it is useful.
>
> I completely appreciate how busy this period is. If a PhD place is not available in your group for 2027, a one-line "not this year" would be very helpful so I can direct my application elsewhere.
>
> Kind regards,
> [Full name]

Two details make this template work:

1. **New value.** You give the supervisor a fresh reason to open it, not just a reminder.
2. **An easy exit.** Asking for a one-line decline dramatically increases reply rates, and even a "not this year" is useful intelligence — it tells you to redirect rather than wait.

## What not to do

- **Don't send a third email.** Two unanswered messages is the limit. A third marks you as a problem applicant before any application is even filed.
- **Don't escalate to the head of department.** This reads as entitled and will be mentioned.
- **Don't send the identical email again.** "Just bumping this" adds nothing and usually gets ignored.
- **Don't take it personally.** Non-reply is an inbox phenomenon, not a verdict on your academic worth.

1. **Contact a second supervisor** whose work overlaps. UK groups usually have several academics who can co-supervise, and the advert often names them.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should I wait before following up?**
10–14 working days. Academic inboxes genuinely back up, and following up after 48 hours signals impatience rather than seriousness.

**Should I follow up in the same email thread or a new one?**
Same thread. It keeps your original message one scroll away and shows continuity.

**What if the supervisor's student or a postdoc is the contact on the advert?**
Email the named contact first — that is who the project expects. Only write to the professor directly if no contact is listed or the listed address bounces.

**Can a non-reply affect my formal application?**
No. If you apply through the official portal, a different process and different people assess you. A quiet inbox is not held against you anywhere.
