**Direct answer box (first 50 words):**

Yes, you can study a Master's in Europe without IELTS at many universities — most commonly through a "medium of instruction" (MOI) certificate if your previous degree was taught in English, or via a university's own interview or internal test. But waivers are per-university, never country-wide, and the visa stage has its own rules.

---

## How "without IELTS" actually works

Nobody waives English. What universities waive is the *standardised test*, substituting other proof that you can function in English. In practice there are four routes:

1. **MOI letter.** Your previous university confirms your degree was taught and examined entirely in English. This is the most common substitute, especially in Germany, Italy and France.
2. **Previous degree from an English-speaking country** (or sometimes "taught in English" anywhere). UK, US, Australian, Irish, Canadian and often Nordic degrees are widely accepted.
3. **University's own assessment.** Some institutions run an online interview or their own English test during admission.
4. **Conditional admission with a pre-sessional language course** before the Master's begins.

What does *not* exist: a blanket "no IELTS needed in country X" policy. Every claim you see online reduces to one of the four routes above, applied by specific universities. That distinction decides whether your application survives scrutiny.

## Germany: the most requested case

Germany deserves its own section because it is the top destination for "without IELTS" searches — and the most misunderstood.

- **MOI waivers are genuinely common.** Many universities accept a certificate from an Indian, Nigerian, Pakistani or Bangladeshi university confirming the entire degree was in English. Some combine this with an interview.
- **But not all do.** TU9 universities and competitive English-taught MSc programmes frequently require IELTS 6.5 / TOEFL 90 equivalents with no exceptions. Check the *programme* page, not the university's general FAQ.
- **The visa stage is the trap.** Germany's student visa does not require IELTS, but the embassy or the university may question an English-medium admission backed by weak documentation. Keep your MOI letter official: on letterhead, signed, stating that the *entire* programme (not just some courses) was in English.
- **Tuition-free or near-free fees mean standards are enforced.** With no fee revenue at stake, German universities have no incentive to bend requirements. Our [guide to studying in Germany for free](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/study-in-germany-for-free-complete-guide-2027) explains what free tuition does and does not cover, and funding options like [DAAD scholarships](https://nextapply.co.uk/scholarship-daad-germany) carry their own language thresholds.

## The MOI letter: how to make it count

A weak MOI letter is the most common reason a "without IELTS" application fails. Make sure yours includes:

- University letterhead and an official signatory (registrar, controller of examinations or equivalent)
- A clear statement that the *entire degree programme* was taught and examined in English
- Your full name, programme name and years of study
- Issue date (recent — many universities want it issued within the last 1–2 years)

If your university refuses to issue an MOI, a waiver through that route is closed. An internal test, interview or a single test sitting is then the honest alternative.

## When "without IELTS" is the wrong goal

Be honest with yourself about trade-offs:

- **If you need a UK visa**, plan for an approved test regardless of waivers — our [UK student visa guide](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/student-visa-uk-2027-financial-requirements-atas-graduate-route) sets out when a Secure English Language Test is mandatory.
- **If your actual English is weak**, skipping the test does not fix the problem — it defers it to a Master's taught entirely in English, where struggling with lectures and academic writing costs far more than a test fee.
- **If an agent promises guaranteed admission without any English proof**, treat it as a red flag: either the university is low-ranked with weak enforcement, or documents are being fabricated — and fabricated MOI letters get offers revoked and visas refused.

A reasonable strategy: shortlist ten universities, mark which accept MOI or interviews, and book one test only if your realistic list collapses below three or four options.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I get a Germany student visa without IELTS?**
Yes. The German student visa does not require IELTS; what matters is a valid admission letter based on your university's accepted proof of English — MOI, internal test or standardised score.

**Is a MOI certificate accepted everywhere?**
No. Acceptance is per-university and per-programme, and some countries' visa processes give MOI less weight than a standardised test. Always confirm with the admissions office in writing.

**Do Nordic or Dutch universities ever waive IELTS?**
Only in narrow cases — usually a previous degree from a majority-English-speaking country. MOI letters alone are rarely sufficient there.

**Can I switch from an MOI-based admission to taking IELTS later?**
You can always take the test and submit a stronger score later, which also helps for scholarships. But an admission based on one proof does not require a second; the reverse — substituting a test with MOI after admission — is rarely allowed.
