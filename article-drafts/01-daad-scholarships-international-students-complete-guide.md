**Direct answer box (first 50 words):**

DAAD is not one scholarship — it is a collection of funding schemes run by the German Academic Exchange Service, each with its own purpose, eligibility rules and deadline. Master's scholarships typically pay €992 per month and doctoral scholarships around €1,400 per month, plus travel, insurance and a study allowance. You apply for the specific scheme, not to "DAAD" in general.

---

## Which DAAD scholarship is right for you?

The single most common mistake applicants make is searching for "the DAAD scholarship." DAAD funds dozens of separate programmes. The three that matter most for international degree-seekers:

| Scheme | Level | Who it's for | Monthly payment |
|---|---|---|---|
| EPOS — Development-Related Postgraduate Courses | Master's | Professionals from developing countries with 2+ years' work experience | €992 + €460 annual study allowance |
| Study Scholarships for Graduates of All Disciplines | Master's | Academically strong graduates applying for a Master's in Germany | €992 |
| Research Grants – Doctoral Programmes | PhD | Graduates planning a doctorate in Germany, often with a supervisor's invitation | €1,400 |

Other schemes cover short-term research stays, summer schools, university summer courses in German, and one-semester exchanges — check the [DAAD scholarship listing on NextApply](https://nextapply.co.uk/scholarship-daad-germany) for the current overview, then confirm everything on the official DAAD portal.

## What DAAD funding actually covers

For the main Master's and PhD schemes (2026–27 rates, verify on the official page before budgeting):

- **Monthly stipend:** €992 (Master's) or €1,400 (PhD), paid for the standard programme duration
- **Study allowance:** €460 per year toward materials and research costs
- **Travel:** one round-trip economy airfare (unless your home institution covers it)
- **Health, accident and liability insurance** for the funding period
- **Optional extras** in some schemes: German language course before the degree, rent subsidy, family allowances

Germany's public universities charge little or no tuition, so the stipend is genuinely designed to cover living costs — that is why DAAD checks your motivation and academic fit more carefully than your bank balance.

## Eligibility: the four checks that decide most outcomes

1. **Degree recency rules.** Most DAAD degree schemes expect your last degree to have been completed no more than six years ago. Count carefully — this rule disqualifies many otherwise strong applicants.
2. **Work experience.** EPOS requires roughly two years of relevant professional experience. Other Master's schemes do not require it, but leadership or professional evidence strengthens your case.
3. **Language requirements.** English-taught programmes usually ask for IELTS 6.0–6.5 or TOEFL equivalents; German-taught ones require certified German levels (often DSH-2 or TestDaF 4). The programme page — not the general DAAD site — defines the exact requirement.
4. **Admission first, or DAAD first?** For most schemes you apply to DAAD while (or before) applying to universities. EPOS applicants apply directly to the course, which then nominates. Check the scheme's "application procedure" section — this varies more than anything else.

## Documents checklist

- DAAD application portal form (submitted online)
- Signed CV in tabular European format (max ~2 pages) — structure it using our [academic CV for PhD applications guide](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/how-to-write-academic-cv-phd-applications-sample); the same principles apply to Master's applications
- Letter of motivation (1–2 pages), specific to the scheme and your career plan
- University transcripts and degree certificates (certified copies)
- Language certificate
- Work references (for EPOS)
- For PhD schemes: a research proposal and often a host supervisor's confirmation — our [PhD research proposal structure guide](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/phd-research-proposal-europe-structure-sample-pdf) shows a worked European example

## What makes DAAD applications succeed

Selection committees consistently reward the same things:

- **A coherent story.** Your previous degree, work and chosen course should form one line of development. DAAD explicitly funds future contributors to their home countries' development — say clearly what you will do with the degree.
- **Specific course choice.** "I found three German universities with this specialisation, and chose X because of module Y" beats any generic praise of German education.
- **Realistic self-assessment.** If your grades are weak, address them once and show compensating evidence (work, projects, publications) rather than hoping nobody notices — see our guide to [applying with a low CGPA](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/low-cgpa-backlogs-study-abroad-guide).
- **Clean, complete documents.** Missing certificates and unsigned forms are among the most common [reasons scholarship applications get rejected](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/why-scholarship-applications-get-rejected-12-reasons).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is DAAD fully funded?**
The main degree schemes cover tuition-free study plus a living stipend, travel and insurance — functionally fully funded. DAAD does not pay tuition at institutions that charge fees for specific programmes; check your course.

**How competitive is DAAD?**
Very. Acceptance rates for popular schemes are estimated in the low double digits, and strong academic records alone are not enough — motivation and development relevance decide ties.

**Can I apply for DAAD and other scholarships together?**
Usually yes, but you must disclose parallel funding and cannot hold two full stipends simultaneously. Check each scheme's rules.

**Do I need German to study in Germany?**
Not for English-taught programmes, though daily life is easier with basic German. Many DAAD schemes include a funded language course before enrolment.

## Official source

All amounts, deadlines and eligibility rules change. Always confirm on **daad.de** before applying, and cross-check the course page of the university itself.
