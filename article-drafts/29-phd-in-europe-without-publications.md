**Direct answer box (first 50 words):**

Yes — you can get a funded PhD in Europe without any publications. Most European doctoral positions, especially employed PhDs in the Netherlands, Germany and Scandinavia, expect zero publications from applicants, because publishing is what the PhD is for. Advertised "desirable" publication records are tiebreakers, not gatekeepers.

---

## Why publications matter less in Europe than applicants fear

Applicants — particularly from systems where a master's thesis publication is common — often assume a blank publications section is disqualifying. In Europe, the opposite is closer to the truth:

- **PhDs are training.** The entire premise of a doctoral position is that you will learn to do research, including publishing, during it. Admissions committees are hiring capability, not a track record.
- **Employed PhDs hire like jobs.** In the Netherlands and Germany, PhDs are salaried staff positions. Selection weighs your thesis quality, methods skills, and fit with the project — the same things a hiring panel weighs for a junior researcher role, where nobody expects prior papers.
- **Publication norms differ by field.** In much of mathematics, social sciences and humanities, publishing before a PhD is genuinely unusual. In computer science and machine learning, preprints are common but far from universal among admitted students.

The exceptions are elite, hyper-competitive pools — top AI labs' doctoral programmes, certain UKRI-funded studentships with hundreds of applicants — where any differentiator helps. Even there, a strong thesis and specific fit routinely beat a thin conference paper.

## What to show instead of publications

Committees assessing research potential without papers look for:

1. **Your master's thesis.** Grade, methods, independence. A distinction thesis with a defensible design is the single best proxy.
2. **Research assistantships and internships.** Paid or credited research work proves you can operate in a lab or archive.
3. **Conference attendance or posters.** Even without a paper, presenting a poster shows you engage with the field's channels.
4. **A preprint or working paper.** Uploading your thesis chapter to a preprint server or university repository makes it quotable — "under review" and "working paper" are legitimate CV entries when done honestly.
5. **A sharp research proposal.** For self-proposed projects, the proposal *is* your publication substitute. Use our [PhD research proposal structure with European example](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/phd-research-proposal-europe-structure-sample-pdf) and give it four weeks, not four days.

## How to present a blank publications section honestly

On your CV, don't fake it and don't apologise for it. Use a section header such as **"Research output"** instead of "Publications", and populate it with:

- Master's thesis (title, grade, one-line contribution)
- Working papers or preprints (labelled accurately)
- Conference posters or talks
- Technical reports, datasets, or software from research work

This reframes the section around evidence of capability. If your grades have gaps too, apply the same principle elsewhere: one honest line of context, then compensating evidence at the top of your [academic CV](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/how-to-write-academic-cv-phd-applications-sample).

## A realistic pre-application plan without publications

If you have 3–6 months before applications and want to strengthen your record:

| Timeline | Action | Yield |
|---|---|---|
| Months 1–2 | Polish the best thesis chapter into a working paper; upload to a repository | Quotable, honest "working paper" |
| Months 1–2 | Ask a supervisor whether a co-authored submission is realistic | Even a workshop paper counts |
| Months 2–4 | Target one workshop or conference (posters have lower bars) | Evidence of engagement |
| Months 2–6 | Draft and refine the research proposal for 2–3 specific projects | Highest-yield item on this list |
| Throughout | Line up references who can speak to your research ability | Decisive in tied decisions |

Notice the proposal sits at the bottom but matters most. Publications help committees choose between candidates; the proposal decides whether you make the shortlist at all — and it decides the timeline too, since good applications need lead time (our [12-month application timeline](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/masters-phd-application-timeline-12-months) sequences it all).

## Frequently asked questions

**Do European PhD programmes require publications?**
No. The overwhelming majority of advertised European PhD positions list publications as "desirable" at most. Formal requirements concern degrees, grades and language.

**Is a published master's thesis worth anything in applications?**
Yes — especially in fields where pre-PhD publishing is rare. Label it accurately (university repository, not peer-reviewed) and highlight the independent contribution it made.

**Should I pay to publish in a journal before applying?**
No. Pay-to-publish outlets with no review process are recognised instantly by academics and can actively hurt credibility. A preprint on a respected server costs nothing and reads better.

**What if my dream programme's alumni all have publications?**
Read the entry requirements, not the alumni's CVs. Requirements define the gate; alumni averages reflect the pool, not the bar. Apply with your best proposal and let the committee compare.
