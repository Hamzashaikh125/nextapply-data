**Direct answer box (first 50 words):**

UK student accommodation costs roughly £400–£900 per month outside London and £900–£1,800+ in London for 2027 entry. University halls are the safest first-year option, private studios cost more but offer independence, and homestay is the cheapest route with meals and cultural immersion included.

## Book early, budget honestly

Two truths shape every accommodation decision: **London is roughly double** most of the rest of the country, and **the best options go earliest** — for September starts, serious searching should begin in spring. Your visa finances also interact with housing: the maintenance requirement (£1,483/month in London, £1,136 outside London at 2026–27 rates — see our [UK student visa financial requirements guide](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/student-visa-uk-2027-financial-requirements-atas-graduate-route)) assumes you can live within it, so pick housing your funds genuinely support.

## University halls: the default for a reason

Halls are owned or contracted by your university and are the standard choice for first-year Master's students and most incoming international students:

- **Guaranteed places** for first-year internationals at many universities, if you apply by their deadline and firmly accept your offer — check your university's terms
- **Bills, internet and contents insurance** typically included, so no surprise costs
- **All-inclusive contracts** aligned to the academic year, often with vacation options
- Apply through your university's accommodation portal once you accept your offer — deadlines cluster in **May–July** for September entry

Downsides: you cannot choose your flatmates, kitchens vary, and premium rooms (ensuite, studio) push prices up sharply. PhD students should note that many universities now offer postgraduate-only halls or family housing with separate application timelines.

## Private student housing: independence at a price

Purpose-built private blocks (the brands with gyms and cinema rooms) and ordinary flatshares fill the private market:

- **Purpose-built studios** offer privacy and bills-inclusive simplicity at premium prices — this is where London's £1,800+ rooms live
- **House/flat shares** with non-student landlords are usually cheapest per room; in most cities £450–£650 outside London
- **Deposits** must legally be protected in a government-approved scheme for assured shorthold tenancies — if your deposit is not protected, that is a red flag
- **Guarantor requirements:** many private landlords require a UK-based guarantor; without one, expect to pay 6–12 months' rent upfront. Ask the university's housing office about guarantor services they support

Watch for scams: never transfer money before viewing a property (or having someone trusted view it), never pay via untraceable methods, and treat listings far below market rate as hostile until proven otherwise.

## Homestay: cheap, structured, and underrated

Living with a host family, arranged through vetted agencies, typically costs £350–£600 per week-equivalent outside London — often with breakfast and dinner included:

- **Cheapest all-in option** for the first months while you learn the city
- **Built-in English practice** and local knowledge — genuinely valuable for new arrivals
- **Limits:** house rules, commutes, and less independence suit some temperaments better than others
- Most homestay agencies are city-based operators; your university's international office will hold a vetted list — start there rather than random web searches

Homestay works best as a **landing pad**: 2–4 months to settle, then move to halls or a flatshare once you know the city and have met people.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do UK universities guarantee accommodation for international students?**
Many guarantee first-year or first-year-postgraduate halls for students who apply by a stated deadline and firmly accept their offer — but the guarantee's terms vary. Check your specific university's accommodation page, not general rankings.

**How much does student accommodation cost in London in 2027?**
Expect roughly £1,000–£1,800+ per month depending on type, with ensuite halls and studios at the top. Budget at least £1,500/month for all living costs; the visa maintenance rate of £1,483/month reflects this.

**Is homestay cheaper than halls?**
Usually yes outside London — £350–£600 with meals included often beats halls once food costs are counted. In London savings are smaller but still real. The trade-offs are independence and commute.

**Can I change accommodation mid-year?**
In halls, typically only in exceptional circumstances. In private tenancies, you may be able to replace yourself with a new tenant or negotiate an early-exit clause — read the contract before signing. Homestay agencies usually allow changes with notice.

## Official source

Housing rules (deposit protection, tenant rights) and prices change; consumer protection differs across England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. **Verify your tenancy rights on gov.uk (private renting guides) and use your university's official accommodation office before signing anything.**
