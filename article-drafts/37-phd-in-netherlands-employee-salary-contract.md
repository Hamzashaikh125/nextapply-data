**Direct answer box (first 50 words):**

In the Netherlands a PhD is a job, not a studentship. Every university PhD candidate is a four-year employee on a national collective scale, paid roughly €2,901 gross per month in year one, rising automatically to about €3,707 by year four (verify the current CAO table). No tuition fees, full employee benefits.

---

## What "employee status" means in practice

The Netherlands is unusual internationally: there is no scholarship route for the standard university PhD (external fellowships aside). You are hired by the university with:

- A **fixed-term employment contract**, typically 4 years (sometimes structured as 18 months + a go/no-go review + an extension)
- A salary from the **national collective labour agreement (CAO) for Dutch universities** — the same scale at every university
- **Pension contributions** via ABP, the civil-servant pension fund
- **Paid holidays** — around 29 days a year plus public holidays
- Sick pay, parental leave and standard Dutch employment protections

This makes the Dutch PhD one of the most secure funded doctorates in Europe — if you can get one of the limited positions. Competition is intense, and many positions are written around a specific project, similar to a job advert.

## The salary scale (2026–27, verify officially)

The CAO prescribes a fixed monthly gross salary through the four years (figures from the recent scale — confirm the current table, as it rises with annual negotiations):

| Year of PhD | Approximate gross/month |
|---|---|
| Year 1 | ~€2,901 |
| Year 2 | ~€3,108 |
| Year 3 | ~€3,325 |
| Year 4 | ~€3,707 |

Notes on these numbers:

- They are **gross, before tax**. Net take-home for a single candidate is typically around €2,200–€2,500/month depending on tax credits and holiday allowance treatment.
- A **holiday allowance (8% of annual salary, paid usually in May)** and an **end-of-year payment (~8.3%, paid in December)** are on top of the monthly figure — don't forget them when budgeting, and don't panic if your December payslip looks different.
- Each university publishes the current CAO table on its HR pages — always verify there or on the **VSNU/UNL** site before signing.

## The contract: what to check before signing

Dutch PhD contracts are standardised, but read yours carefully for:

1. **Duration split.** Many contracts run 4 years outright; some start with 18 months, then extend after the go/no-go (see below). If your contract is split, the extension criteria should be documented.
2. **The go/no-go (go/no-go assessment).** After roughly 9–12 months you present a research plan and first results; the team decides whether to continue. Failures are rare but real — prepare seriously.
3. **Teaching load.** Dutch PhD candidates often teach a few hours a week; the CAO caps this (research must remain the majority of your time). Check the teaching clause.
4. **Training and supervision plan.** You and your supervisor formally agree a plan in year one — courses, conferences, milestones. It's a contract annex, not a courtesy.
5. **Notice periods and residence implications.** Your employment contract underpins your residence permit — see the visa section below.

## Is €2,901 enough to live on?

For a single person in a mid-sized Dutch city, yes — modestly. The catch is not food or transport but **housing**: the national shortage makes finding a room or flat in Amsterdam, Utrecht or Delft genuinely difficult, and rooms in the tightest markets can consume €700–€1,000+ of a €2,300 net salary. Start the housing search before you sign, and check our [cost-of-living breakdown for the Netherlands, Germany and the UK](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/cost-of-living-phd-students-netherlands-germany-uk-2027) for per-city monthly budgets.

Practical tips from candidates:

- University housing is offered only to some internationals, often for the first year only — read your offer letter.
- Smaller cities (Wageningen, Twente, Groningen, Tilburg) are far easier on both housing and daily costs.

## Visa and residence for international PhD employees

Because you're an employee, your residence permit follows employment rules rather than student rules. Dutch universities sponsor PhD hires, typically through the **recognised sponsor** (erkend referent) system or the highly skilled migrant/researcher routes — the university's HR department handles the application. Key points:

- You generally don't apply for a student visa for a salaried PhD.
- Family members can usually accompany you with work rights.
- After the PhD, the **orientation year** gives you 12 months to find work in the Netherlands.

Confirm the exact route with your hiring university's HR and **ind.nl** — procedures and thresholds change.

## How to actually get one of these positions

- **Search project-based vacancies.** Dutch PhD positions are advertised like jobs on university career sites and academic job boards — with a project description, supervisor name and closing date. Read the project description carefully and mirror its language in your application.
- **Apply with a CV and motivation letter** tuned to the project, not a generic academic application — your application documents should argue why *this* project fits your trajectory.
- **Expect an interview**, often with a presentation or a research-task component.

External funding routes exist too (e.g. personal fellowships or embassy scholarships), but the standard employment contract is the norm.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do PhD students in the Netherlands pay tuition fees?**
No. As employees they don't pay tuition. (External/contract PhD candidates outside an employment contract are the exception — check your specific arrangement.)

**Is the Dutch PhD salary taxed?**
Yes — gross figures (~€2,901–€3,707/month) are before income tax and social contributions. Net is typically €2,200–€2,500/month depending on personal circumstances. Verify with a Dutch tax calculator for your situation.

**Can the university fire me after the go/no-go?**
The assessment is a genuine gate: if the team judges the trajectory not viable, the contract may not be extended past the initial phase. It's uncommon with honest supervision, but take the first-year plan seriously.

**Can I do a Dutch PhD part-time or externally?**
Part-time employed positions are rare; "external PhD candidates" (buitenpromovendus) do a doctorate without employment and without salary — usually self- or employer-funded. Most international applicants should target the salaried employee route.

## Official source

Salary scales are renegotiated annually. Always verify current amounts in the official **CAO Nederlandse Universiteiten** table (published by UNL) and on your university's HR pages before making decisions or signing a contract.
