## Why this comparison decides where you study

Post-study work rights are the single biggest difference between study destinations on paper — and often the deciding factor in practice. Three years of open work rights (UK PhD) is a different career proposition from one year of field-limited employment (US OPT). The table below is the 2027 snapshot; every one of these schemes is politically contested, so verify before you commit.

## UK Graduate Route: the freedom option

You apply from inside the UK after completing your course, on a valid Student visa. There is no job offer, no salary threshold and no restriction on employer or role — you can freelance, job-hunt or stack temporary work. The doctoral advantage is real: **3 years vs 2** for taught graduates. For the application mechanics and the financial requirements of the visa that precedes it, see our [UK student visa financial requirements guide](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/student-visa-uk-2027-financial-requirements-atas-graduate-route) and the [step-by-step UK visa application guide](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/how-to-apply-uk-student-visa-step-by-step-2026-2027).

The catch: the Graduate Route is a bridge, not a destination. To stay beyond it you must switch to a Skilled Worker visa, which does require a sponsoring employer and a salary threshold. The scheme's length and conditions are also under periodic government review — budget for the possibility of change.

## US OPT: powerful but conditional

OPT (Optional Practical Training) lets F-1 graduates work in the US for **12 months in a job directly related to their field of study**. STEM graduates on the 2025–27 designated-degree lists can apply for a **24-month extension**, reaching 36 months total. Constraints that trip people up:

- Employment must relate to your degree field; unemployment over 90 days (150 with the extension) can end your status
- Your university's DSO must recommend it, and reporting duties continue throughout
- Staying longer requires an H-1B — a lottery that roughly one in three applicants won in recent cycles

For PhD graduates in STEM, 36 months is generous, but it is field-locked and employer-policied in a way the Graduate Route is not.

## Canada, Australia, Germany at a glance

- **Canada (PGWP):** up to 3 years depending on programme length, any employer — but eligibility rules and field restrictions have shifted recently; check current criteria carefully. Its real pull is the PR pathway behind it (see our [PR pathways comparison](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/pr-pathways-after-study-canada-australia-germany-uk)).
- **Australia (485):** 2–3 years with any employer; regional study adds options. Recent changes have tightened age limits and English requirements.
- **Germany:** no dedicated post-study work permit — instead, graduates get a residence permit to seek qualified work, and PhD graduates move quickly onto EU Blue Card terms. Combined with no tuition at public universities, the total package can beat Anglophone options — see [studying in Germany for free](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/study-in-germany-for-free-complete-guide-2027).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the Graduate Route the same as the old PSW visa?**
Functionally yes — the Graduate Route replaced the old Tier 1 (Post-Study Work) scheme in 2021 with the same core promise: open work rights for a fixed period. The PhD extension to 3 years is the doctoral advantage.

**Can I apply for OPT and the Graduate Route?**
You cannot hold both simultaneously — they attach to study in different countries. The comparison is about choosing a destination, not stacking visas.

**Does the Graduate Route count toward UK settlement?**
Years on the Graduate Route generally do not count toward the qualifying period for settlement, which typically starts when you switch to a work visa. Plan your timeline accordingly — see our [PR pathways after study](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/pr-pathways-after-study-canada-australia-germany-uk) guide.

**Which is better for PhD graduates, UK or US?**
The UK offers 3 years of fully open work rights and no lottery; the US offers deep-field salaries but a field-locked OPT and an H-1B lottery afterwards. If certainty matters most, the UK wins; if maximum earnings and you are STEM-listed, the US remains compelling.

## Official source

All five schemes are under active political review and change frequently. **Verify durations, fees and eligibility on gov.uk (Graduate Route), USCIS (OPT), IRCC (PGWP), Home Affairs Australia (485) and the German Auswärtiges Amt before making decisions.**
