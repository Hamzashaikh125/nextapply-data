**Direct answer box (first 50 words):**

If a scholarship deadline has passed, you generally cannot submit a late application — but you still have options: check whether the scheme accepts late or rolling submissions, email the funder directly, apply to scholarships with later deadlines, or plan deliberately for the next cycle with a stronger file.

---

## First: confirm the deadline actually passed

Deadlines move more often than applicants expect. Before writing anything off:

- **Time zones matter.** Many portals close at 23:59 in the funder's local time, not yours. A "missed" deadline may have hours left.
- **Check for extensions.** Funders sometimes extend deadlines by a few weeks — look at the official page and the funder's news or social media feed, not just your saved screenshot.
- **Different deadlines per route.** DAAD schemes, Chevening, Commonwealth and university-run scholarships all close on different dates, even when funding the same intake. If one door closed, another may still be open — the [DAAD scholarship listing on NextApply](https://nextapply.co.uk/scholarship-daad-germany) is a good place to see which scheme fits which calendar.

## 5 things you can still do

| # | Action | What it actually looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Email the funder | A short, polite message asking whether late submissions are accepted. Usually the answer is no, but some schemes hold reserve lists or accept late applications when places are unfilled. |
| 2 | Find later-deadline schemes | Shift to scholarships whose windows are still open, or to funded positions (most European PhD posts advertise with rolling deadlines). |
| 3 | Use rolling-review funders | Some university scholarships review applications as they arrive until funds run out — earlier is better, but "closed" is not a fixed date. |
| 4 | Ask about the next cycle | Confirm the next round's dates now and what (if anything) will change. Deadlines rarely move by more than a few weeks year to year. |
| 5 | Strengthen the file meanwhile | Use the extra months for a better research proposal, a publication, language scores or work experience — the things that actually move decisions. |

## Why "just submit late anyway" usually fails

It is tempting to send the application regardless. Resist. Most portals physically close; where they don't, an application that ignores a stated deadline signals exactly the kind of carelessness that gets applications rejected — see our breakdown of the [12 most common reasons scholarship applications fail](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/why-scholarship-applications-get-rejected-12-reasons). Funders read hundreds of eligible files; they have no reason to bend rules for one ineligible entry.

The one exception worth trying: genuine technical or medical emergencies, documented. A portal crash on the closing day, or a hospital stay, is worth a factual email with evidence. Funders deal with these cases rarely but not never.

## Replanning around the next cycle

If the deadline is truly gone, turn the loss into a schedule. Most major schemes run on predictable annual cycles:

- **Chevening** (UK Master's): applications typically open in August and close in early November.
- **Commonwealth Master's/PhD scholarships**: usually open late in the year for the following academic year.
- **DAAD degree schemes**: many deadlines fall between August and October, more than a year before study starts.
- **Gates Cambridge and Rhodes**: tied to specific university application rounds — apply early in that window.

Build the whole sequence backwards from the earliest deadline using our [12-month application timeline](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/masters-phd-application-timeline-12-months). Most missed deadlines are not bad luck — they are the result of starting after the references, tests and certificates were already needed.

## If the degree matters more than the scholarship

Consider a parallel track: a funded PhD position (common in Germany, the Netherlands and Scandinavia) or a modestly funded programme where you cover part of the cost. If you are weighing self-funding against waiting a year, read our guide to [what a self-funded PhD really costs and the alternatives](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/what-does-self-funded-phd-mean-costs-funding-alternatives). A gap year spent working in your field is not a failure — it often makes the next application stronger, especially for development-oriented schemes.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I submit a scholarship application after the deadline?**
Usually no. Portals close automatically and most funders state that late applications are not considered. A short email asking is harmless and occasionally reveals a reserve list or extended window — but do not count on it.

**Do scholarship deadlines ever get extended?**
Yes, sometimes — by days or a few weeks, and usually announced on the funder's own website and social channels. Never rely on an extension existing; plan for the published date.

**Is there any point emailing the scholarship provider?**
Yes, if you have a genuine documented emergency, or a specific question about the next round. Keep it factual and brief. Generic "please let me apply late" emails rarely change anything.

**What should I do in the months until the next round opens?**
Fix the weak points of your last application: retake a language test, add research or work experience, redraft your proposal, and secure stronger references early.

## Official source

Deadlines, extension announcements and eligibility rules change every cycle. Always verify dates on the funder's **official website** — never on third-party blogs or reposts — before you build your plan around them.
