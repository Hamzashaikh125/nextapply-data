**Direct answer box (first 50 words):**

Email a UK PhD supervisor with a short (150–200 word), specific message: one line on who you are, two or three sentences showing you have actually read their work, a clear question about whether they are taking students, and your CV attached. Send it Tuesday–Thursday morning; follow up once after two weeks.

---

## Why most "cold emails" to supervisors fail

Supervisors receive dozens of generic enquiries every term. The ones that get deleted share the same faults: they never mention a specific paper or project, they ask the supervisor to "guide me on funding opportunities" (that is your job), and they run to 600 words.

The ones that get replies do three things in under 200 words:

1. **Name the project.** Ideally an advertised studentship, or a funding scheme you could realistically bring — our guide to [spotting a PhD that is genuinely funded for international students](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/how-to-identify-funded-phd-accepts-international-students) shows how to verify this before you write.
2. **Prove you read their work.** One specific sentence beats a paragraph of flattery.
3. **Ask one answerable question.** "Are you accepting a PhD student for October 2027 on X topic?" can be answered in five seconds.

Before you send anything, make sure your CV is ready — supervisors often forward promising emails straight to admissions, and a weak CV kills the thread. Our [academic CV structure guide with example](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/how-to-write-academic-cv-phd-applications-sample) covers the two-page academic format.

## The 7 templates

Adjust the bracketed placeholders and keep every email under 200 words.

### Template 1 — First contact about an advertised project

> **Subject:** Enquiry: [project title] PhD — October 2027 start
>
> Dear Dr [Surname],
>
> I am writing about the PhD project "[project title]" advertised on [FindAPhD / university website], for which you are the primary supervisor. I recently completed my [MSc/BS] in [subject] at [university], where my dissertation on [topic] used [method].
>
> Your group's work on [specific paper or project] directly relates to my interest in [one-line link]. In particular, [one specific observation or question about their work].
>
> I have attached my CV and transcript. Would you be taking students on this project for October 2027, and is there anything useful I could send ahead of a formal application?
>
> Kind regards,
> [Full name]

### Template 2 — Self-proposed topic (no advert exists)

> **Subject:** PhD enquiry — [your topic in 5 words]
>
> Dear Dr [Surname],
>
> I am exploring PhD options for 2027 and your work on [theme] is the closest match I have found to my proposed topic: [one-sentence topic].
>
> My background: [degree], [year], plus [one relevant achievement — job, publication, thesis grade]. My draft proposal outline is attached (2 pages). If this falls within your current interests, I would welcome 15 minutes to discuss whether a self-funded or studentship route might work — I note your group advertises projects under [scheme, if known].
>
> Would you be open to a brief conversation?
>
> Kind regards,
> [Full name]

### Template 3 — Asking about funding (before you invest months)

> **Subject:** Funding question — [project title] PhD
>
> Dear Dr [Surname],
>
> Thank you for your earlier reply confirming interest in my application. Before I finalise my research proposal, could I clarify the funding situation? Specifically: is the project tied to the [studentship name], and are international applicants eligible, or is it limited to home students?
>
> If the studentship is home-only, I would be grateful for any pointers to schemes you have seen international students in your group use. I am currently looking at [scholarship name] as a parallel route.
>
> Kind regards,
> [Full name]

### Template 4 — Explaining a gap or weak grade, briefly

> **Subject:** Follow-up: PhD enquiry — [topic]
>
> Dear Dr [Surname],
>
> Following my email of [date], I wanted to add one line of context: my undergraduate average of [X]% reflects [one honest sentence — illness, working 20 hours/week, early-stage adjustment]. Since then I have [MSc distinction / certification / project], which I believe better represents my current ability.
>
> I remain very interested in [project title] and would welcome any feedback on whether my profile could be competitive.
>
> Kind regards,
> [Full name]

One sentence, no excuses, then evidence. If the weakness is bigger than one line, let the evidence carry it: put compensating achievements at the top of your [academic CV](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/how-to-write-academic-cv-phd-applications-sample) so the committee reads them first.

### Template 5 — Requesting a quick call

> **Subject:** 15-minute call? — PhD enquiry, [topic]
>
> Dear Dr [Surname],
>
> Thank you for your reply. I have read [paper/proposal] as suggested and drafted the attached one-page outline accordingly. I have two focused questions — about [Q1] and [Q2] — that are quicker to resolve by voice than email.
>
> Would you have 15 minutes in the next two weeks? I can fit around your schedule and am available [two concrete windows, with time zone].
>
> Kind regards,
> [Full name]

### Template 6 — After a positive reply / before formal application

> **Subject:** Next steps — application for October 2027
>
> Dear Dr [Surname],
>
> Thank you — very helpful. As suggested, I will submit through [portal] by [deadline]. My proposal now reflects your point about [specific change], and I have confirmed [referee names] are able to provide references.
>
> One last question: would you like to see a final draft of the proposal before I submit, or shall I apply directly?
>
> Kind regards,
> [Full name]

### Template 7 — After interview or rejection, keeping the door open

> **Subject:** Thank you — [project title] interview
>
> Dear Dr [Surname],
>
> Thank you for the conversation on [date]. I particularly enjoyed your point about [specific topic] — it has sharpened how I think about [theme].
>
> If a future studentship opens in your group, I would be glad to be considered. Meanwhile, I will proceed with applications elsewhere, including [scheme], where your earlier advice on [point] has been very useful.
>
> With thanks,
> [Full name]

Rejections are normal — what you do in the following week matters more. See our guide on [what to do after a PhD application is rejected](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/phd-application-rejected-what-next).

## Timing, etiquette and follow-up

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Send Tue–Thu, 8–10am UK time | Send Friday evening or at 3am local time |
| Follow up once after 10–14 working days | Follow up twice in a week |
| Use a university or professional email address | Use an informal personal address |
| Attach CV + transcript as PDFs, clearly named | Attach 6 files or send a Drive link with no context |
| CC nobody on first contact | CC three supervisors in one email |

If a week or two passes with no reply, don't take it personally — inboxes flood during admissions season. Our [follow-up guide with a ready template](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/how-to-follow-up-phd-supervisor-does-not-reply) shows exactly when and how to nudge.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should an email to a PhD supervisor be?**
150–200 words. Long enough to prove you have read their work; short enough to be read on a phone between meetings.

**Should I email multiple supervisors at once?**
Yes, but separately and with individually tailored messages. Never send one identical email to several supervisors, and never CC them together — UK academia is small.

**What if I get no reply at all?**
Send one follow-up after two weeks. If that fails, move on — non-replies usually mean the supervisor is away, full, or the mailbox is unmonitored. Silence is not a judgment on your ability.

**Do I need a supervisor before applying to a UK PhD?**
For most UK PhDs, yes — an informal "yes, I'd support your application" from a supervisor is effectively step one, before the formal portal application. In the Netherlands and much of continental Europe, you apply to an advertised project instead, where the advert itself is the first contact.
