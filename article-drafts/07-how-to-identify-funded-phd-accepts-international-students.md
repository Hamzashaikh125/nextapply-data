**Direct answer box (first 50 words):**

A PhD is genuinely fully funded for international students only if the offer covers tuition fees in full *and* pays you a living stipend or salary, with no nationality restriction. Check the advert's funding statement, the fee status rules, and the eligibility page — three places where "funded" quietly stops being true.

---

## Why "funded PhD" needs verification

The phrase "fully funded PhD" is used loosely. Adverts say it when funding exists for *home* students only, when "funding available" means a competitive scholarship you must win separately, or when the stipend does not actually meet visa maintenance rules. Before investing weeks in an application, run the seven checks below. Our full guide on [reading a PhD job advertisement before applying](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/how-to-read-phd-job-advertisement-before-applying) goes deeper on the advert itself; this article focuses on the funding question.

## The 7-point funding check

| # | Check | Where to look | What "good" looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fees covered? | Advert + project page | Explicit sentence: "fees at the international/international-graduate rate are covered" |
| 2 | Stipend or salary? | Same paragraph | A stated amount, e.g. UKRI-rate stipend (~£19,000–£20,000/year; verify current rate) or a salary (TV-L E13 in Germany) |
| 3 | Duration of funding | Advert small print | Funding for the full 3–4 years, not "year 1 in the first instance" |
| 4 | Nationality restrictions | Eligibility section | "Open to all nationalities" — or an explicit list including yours |
| 5 | Who awards the money | Advert footer | A named funder (UKRI, university, DAAD, ERC project) — not "subject to funding being secured" |
| 6 | Application route | How to apply | One application covers admission + funding; or a clearly stated separate scholarship step with a deadline |
| 7 | Visa maintenance compliance | University visa guidance | Stipend meets the government's maintenance threshold (verify on official immigration pages) |

If any row fails, you have found the catch. If rows 1, 2 and 4 all pass, you are almost certainly looking at a genuinely funded position.

## Red-flag wording, decoded

- **"Funding available for outstanding candidates"** → There is a scholarship pool, most applicants will not get it. Ask the department what share of students were funded last year.
- **"Self-funded students welcome to apply"** → No money attached at all. Read our guide on [what a self-funded PhD means and its alternatives](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/what-does-self-funded-phd-mean-costs-funding-alternatives) before considering it.
- **"Home fees only"** or **"home students only"** → International students either pay the fee gap themselves or are excluded. In the UK, "home" vs "international" fee status has its own rules — check the university's fee status assessment guidance.
- **"Stipend at UKRI rate"** → Good sign, but confirm the project actually has the studentship, not that the department "hopes" to have one.
- **"Competitive scholarship required"** → You are applying for admission, then separately for money. Plan for both deadlines — and a fallback.

## Country shortcuts: where funded means funded

| Country | Default funding model | International access |
|---|---|---|
| Netherlands, Nordics | PhD = salaried employment | Open to all nationalities; strong access |
| Germany | Salaried positions or DAAD-type scholarships | Open; verify each advert |
| UK | Studentships (UKRI, charities, universities) | Many projects open internationally, but some fund home fees only — check row 1 and 4 carefully |
| USA | Assistantships bundled with admission | Open at research universities; an unfunded offer in STEM research is a warning sign |
| Australia | RTP scholarships + university awards | Competitive but internationally open |

The general pattern: in much of continental Europe, funded is the default; in the UK and Australia, you must verify project by project. ## What to do when the advert is ambiguous

1. **Email the contact named in the advert.** One factual question: "Is the studentship open to international applicants, and does it cover international-level fees plus a stipend for the full duration?" This also functions as a first contact — see our [email templates for PhD supervisors](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/how-to-email-phd-supervisor-uk-7-templates).
2. **Check the funder's own page**, not just the university's. ERC projects, UKRI doctoral training partnerships and charities publish their own eligibility, including nationality rules.
3. **Ask current students.** Departmental social media or the grad school office can tell you what share of last year's intake was funded — more honest than any advert.
4. **Line up parallel routes.** Named scholarships — Gates Cambridge (stipend ~£18,744; verify current figures), Rhodes (~£21,060; verify), Commonwealth, DAAD (doctoral rate €1,400/month; verify) — run on separate calendars. Browse the [Gates Cambridge listing](https://nextapply.co.uk/gates-cambridge-scholarship-2027-fully-funded) or the [Rhodes listing](https://nextapply.co.uk/rhodes-scholarship-2027-oxford-fully-funded) to compare terms, and sequence everything with our [12-month application timeline](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/masters-phd-application-timeline-12-months).

## Frequently asked questions

**Does "fully funded" always include international tuition fees?**
No. Many UK studentships cover home fees only, leaving international students to pay the difference. Only a sentence explicitly covering international/international-graduate fees counts as fully funded for you.

**Can international students get UKRI-funded PhDs?**
Yes — UKRI-funded studentships are open to international applicants, and international students' fees can be covered, but places funded at the international fee level are limited and very competitive. Check each doctoral training partnership's rules.

**Is a salaried PhD position better than a scholarship?**
Often yes: positions (Netherlands, Nordics, Germany) pay a full salary with benefits, have no separate "win the award" step, and treat you as staff. Scholarships can be more prestigious and sometimes portable, but they are fewer and more competitive.

**What questions should I ask a supervisor about funding?**
Ask four: Is the position funded from day one? For how many years? Does the funding cover international-level fees? Is there any nationality restriction on the funder's side?

## Official source

Stipend rates, fee levels, visa maintenance rules and funder eligibility change annually and vary by scheme. Always verify against the **official advert, the funder's own page and the university's fee/visa guidance** before applying — never rely on an advert summary or a third-party listing (including ours) as the final word.
