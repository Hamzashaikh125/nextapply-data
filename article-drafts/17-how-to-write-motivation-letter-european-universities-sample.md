**Direct answer box (first 50 words):**

A motivation letter for a European university explains three things in 1–2 pages: why this field, why this programme, and what you will do with the degree afterwards. It is more personal and forward-looking than a cover letter. Write it as five paragraphs — background, academic interest, programme fit, career plan, close.

---

## What European universities actually want from a motivation letter

Continental European admissions — Germany, the Netherlands, the Nordics, France, Italy, Central Europe — tend to be more direct than UK admissions. They are not looking for literary flair. They want a coherent, plausible account of:

1. **Where you come from academically** (degree, key projects)
2. **Why this programme specifically** (named modules, research groups, structure)
3. **What happens next** (a career or research plan that the programme obviously serves)

German and Dutch committees in particular reward the third point. A letter that says "this Master's in Water Management leads to my goal of working in transboundary river-basin governance" is stronger than one that says "I have always been passionate about water". Vague passion is the most common failure mode.

UK applicants note: a motivation letter is closer to a UK personal statement than a cover letter. Our guide to the [UK Master's personal statement](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/masters-personal-statement-uk-examples) covers that variant; and if you are unsure which document a programme is asking for, our [SOP vs personal statement vs research proposal comparison](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/sop-vs-personal-statement-vs-research-proposal) untangles the terminology.

## The five-paragraph structure

| Paragraph | Content | Length |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook + statement of purpose | What you are applying for and the one-line story of why | 3–4 sentences |
| 2. Academic background | Degree, strongest project or thesis, key skills — with specifics | 1 short para |
| 3. Why this programme | Named modules, research groups, labs, teaching style — never generic praise | 1 short para |
| 4. Career / research plan | Concrete next step the programme enables | 1 short para |
| 5. Close | Availability, thanks, one memorable line tying it together | 2 sentences |

For PhD applications, paragraph 3 becomes "why this research group and supervisor", and the whole letter leans toward your proposed research — pair it with our [PhD research proposal structure](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/phd-research-proposal-europe-structure-sample-pdf).

## Sample paragraphs (adapt, don't copy)

**Opening (paragraph 1):**

> I am writing to apply for the MSc Environmental Engineering at TU Dresden. After four years of civil engineering in Lagos, including two flood-relief deployments, I want to move from building drainage systems to designing the city-scale water models that decide where those systems go — and Dresden's programme is one of the few in Europe that teaches both the modelling and the municipal practice.

**Academic background (paragraph 2):**

> My BSc in Civil Engineering at the University of Lagos (Second Class Upper) culminated in a final-year project modelling stormwater runoff for the Ojota district using SWMM, for which I collected my own rainfall and topography data across six field visits. The project received the department's best-thesis award and taught me that data scarcity, not modelling complexity, is the real constraint in my region's flood planning.

**Why this programme (paragraph 3):**

> I am applying to Dresden specifically for the Urban Water Management specialisation: the modules in hydro-informatics and integrated flood risk directly extend my SWMM work, and Prof. [name]'s research group on resilient drainage systems addresses the exact scale — informal urban settlements — my fieldwork could not reach. The programme's semester-long project placement would let me test model outputs against real municipal data.

**Career plan (paragraph 4):**

> After graduation I intend to return to water-infrastructure planning in West Africa, initially with a river-basin authority and longer-term in flood-policy consulting. The MSc's combination of modelling tools and European regulatory exposure is the missing layer between my field experience and that goal.

Each paragraph does one job and contains at least one concrete noun — a module, a place, a number, a name. That is the difference between a letter that reads as written-by-you and one that reads as written-by-template.

## Country differences worth knowing

| Country | Expectation |
|---|---|
| Germany | Formal, structured, career-plan heavy; uni-assist applications often specify length and topics to address |
| Netherlands | Direct and concrete; motivation plus fit with the specific track; modesty reads as weakness more than as virtue |
| Nordics | Emphasis on independence and academic readiness; the "why this programme" paragraph is essential |
| France / Italy / Spain | Similar structure; length usually capped at one page |
| Switzerland | Formal tone, precision valued; for EPFL/ETH follow their stated content checklist exactly |

Always check the programme page for a stated structure. If the university lists questions to address, treat them as your paragraph plan — committees grade against exactly those questions.

## Common mistakes

- **Recycling one letter across universities.** Paragraph 3 must be rewritten every time; panels can smell a find-and-replace.
- **Narrating your CV chronologically.** "First I did X, then Y, then Z" wastes the letter. Select the two experiences that matter and interpret them.
- **Flattery without evidence.** "Your prestigious university" — no. "Your module in X, which matches my thesis on Y" — yes.
- **No future.** Letters with no career plan read as if the applicant wants a degree rather than what the degree leads to.
- **Ignoring stated limits.** If the portal says 800 words or one page, exceeding it is a compliance fail, not a flourish.

## Practical production tips

- Draft in plain text first, format second.
- Read it aloud; every sentence you stumble over is a sentence to cut.
- Ask one person who got into a similar programme to critique it — not for style, but for whether the "why this programme" paragraph convinces.
- Export as PDF named `Surname-MotivationLetter-Programme.pdf`.
- Start four weeks before the deadline. A good motivation letter takes three serious drafts — our [12-month application timeline](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/masters-phd-application-timeline-12-months) shows where this fits in the wider plan.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should a motivation letter be?**
One page (roughly 400–500 words) unless the programme specifies otherwise. Some German uni-assist applications ask for up to two pages — follow their number, not folklore.

**Is a motivation letter the same as a personal statement?**
Close, but not identical. A UK personal statement dwells more on your development and intellectual curiosity; a European motivation letter is more explicit about the programme choice and career outcome. Our [comparison of SOP, personal statement and research proposal](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/sop-vs-personal-statement-vs-research-proposal) maps the differences.

**Can I mention financial reasons for applying?**
Briefly and pragmatically if relevant (e.g., tuition-free study enabling you to study at all), but never as the main motivation. Lead with academic and career reasons; check [fully funded options on NextApply](https://nextapply.co.uk/fully-funded-scholarships) to reduce that pressure.

**Should I address weaknesses in my grades in the letter?**
One sentence, factual, followed immediately by compensating evidence. Dwelling hurts; silence can look evasive if the weakness is obvious.
