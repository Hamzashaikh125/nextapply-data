**Direct answer box (first 50 words):**

There is no universal formula for converting CGPA to UK degree classifications. Each UK university maintains its own international equivalence tables and applies them to your transcript in the context of your home institution's grading system. Roughly: a 3.3–3.7 GPA often maps to a UK 2:1, and above 3.7 to a first.

---

## Why no official conversion exists

UK degrees are classified — First, Upper Second (2:1), Lower Second (2:2), Third — and international applicants are mapped onto that scale. But the mapping is done by each university, not by a central body, because grading cultures differ too much for one formula to be fair:

- In some systems a 3.5/4.0 is exceptional; in others it is ordinary.
- Percentage-based systems (India, Nigeria, Pakistan) vary enormously between institutions and boards — a 65% from one university can outrank a 75% from another.
- Some systems grade harshly and publish class positions or medians, which a converter ignores entirely.

This is why the same transcript can be judged a UK 2:1 by one university and 2:2 by another. Both are being honest; they are using different equivalence tables.

## An illustrative conversion table

Use this only to estimate where you stand. It reflects common patterns used by UK admissions teams for well-known grading systems — not an official rule.

| Your result (typical system) | Illustrative UK equivalent | What it usually means for Master's entry |
|---|---|---|
| GPA 3.7–4.0 / ~85%+ (India: 75%+ or CGPA 9+) | First-class honours | Meets requirements at almost any university |
| GPA 3.3–3.6 / 70–84% (India: 60–74%) | Upper second (2:1) | Meets the standard requirement at most universities |
| GPA 2.8–3.2 / 60–69% | Lower second (2:2) | Accepted by many universities, especially those asking for "2:2 or equivalent" |
| GPA 2.4–2.7 / 50–59% | Third class | Limited options; look at 2:2-friendly routes or pre-master's courses |

Three caveats matter more than the numbers:

1. **Check the university's own country page.** Most UK universities publish country-specific requirements ("India: bachelor's degree with 55–60% from a recognised university" and so on). That page overrides any generic table — including this one.
2. **Conversion is per-institution, not per-country.** Universities familiar with your institution's grading culture may judge you more generously (or strictly) than the national average suggests.
3. **They may convert your raw transcript themselves.** Some universities recalculate your average using only relevant modules, which can move you up or down a band.

## How this works for PhD applications

For PhDs the classification matters less than you might expect. A doctoral place in the UK is offered by a supervisor and department, not by a central admissions formula, so:

- A 2:1 plus a strong Master's (Merit or Distinction) is a standard profile; an excellent Master's result can offset a modest undergraduate record.
- Funded positions are more competitive and often specify "2:1 or equivalent" as a formal minimum — but research fit, proposal quality and supervisor backing routinely outweigh a borderline grade.
- Before fixating on eligibility, check whether the position is genuinely funded for your nationality — our guide on [identifying funded PhDs that accept international students](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/how-to-identify-funded-phd-accepts-international-students) walks through the checks.

## Practical steps to find your real equivalent

1. **Find the course's stated requirement** — usually "2:1 or equivalent in a relevant subject."
2. **Open the university's international entry requirements page for your country.** Look for your grading system by name.
3. **If unclear, email admissions** with your transcript and one direct question: "Does my average meet the 2:1 equivalence for this course?" Most universities answer, and a written reply is useful evidence if it is yes.
4. **Do not rely on online GPA converters.** Generic calculators have no idea how your institution grades.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a 3.0 GPA a 2:1 in the UK?**
Usually not — 3.0 typically falls at or near the 2:2 boundary. But some universities accept 3.0 as 2:1-equivalent from certain systems and institutions, so check the specific university's country page before ruling anything out.

**Do UK universities use WES or other evaluation services?**
Rarely for admissions decisions. WES evaluations matter mostly for North America. UK universities do their own conversions against their published country requirements.

**Can I apply for a PhD with a 2:2?**
Occasionally, especially with a strong Master's, publications, or substantial relevant experience. Some universities state a hard 2:1 minimum for funded places; self-funded routes can be more flexible. Our guide on [what a self-funded PhD involves](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/what-does-self-funded-phd-mean-costs-funding-alternatives) explains the trade-offs before you commit.

**Does my final-year average count more?**
Sometimes. Some universities weight later years more heavily, and some ask only for your final-year average for postgraduate entry. This varies — another reason to ask admissions directly.
