Most PhD researchers in Germany are paid under the TV-L E13 collective agreement — but your actual salary depends on three things: your pay group (E13), your contract percentage (usually 50–75% of full time) and your pay step (which rises with seniority). A 100% E13 position starts around €4,600–€4,800 gross per month.

---

## First, understand the three dimensions of your pay

German public-sector academic pay confuses everyone at first because "E13" is only one of three variables:

1. **Pay group (Entgeltgruppe):** E13 is the standard group for academic staff with a university degree — doctoral researchers and postdocs. It says nothing on its own about what you'll earn.
2. **Contract percentage (Dienstvereinbarung/Bemessungssatz):** Most PhD positions are not full-time. A "50% position" pays half the E13 table salary; 65% and 75% are also common in STEM. The contract states the percentage explicitly — read it before signing.
3. **Pay step (Stufe):** Within E13, salary rises automatically through steps (1–6) based on years of relevant work experience. New PhD graduates start at Stufe 1–2; prior work experience can place you higher.

So when someone asks "what does a PhD pay in Germany?", the honest answer is: *it depends on the percentage* — which is why ranges vary so much online.

## What E13 actually pays (2026–27, verify officially)

The TV-L tables differ slightly by state (Bundesland), since TV-L is a collective agreement for most states while Berlin, Hesse and a few others run their own versions. Approximate monthly gross figures:

| Contract | Approximate gross/month | Approximate gross/year |
|---|---|---|
| 100% E13, starting step | ~€4,600–€4,800 | ~€55,000–€58,000 |
| 75% E13 | ~€3,450–€3,600 | ~€41,000–€43,000 |
| 65% E13 | ~€3,000–€3,100 | ~€36,000–€37,000 |
| 50% E13 | ~€2,300–€2,400 | ~€28,000–€29,000 |

Net take-home is typically 55–60% of gross depending on your tax class, church tax and whether you opt out of public health insurance. A 65% position might net roughly €2,000–€2,200/month in Tax Class I — comfortably liveable in most German university cities, as our [cost-of-living comparison](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/cost-of-living-phd-students-netherlands-germany-uk-2027) shows.

Always check the current official table: the **GES/entgelttabelle** listings or your university's HR page state the exact figure for your state and step. Figures rise with regular collective-bargaining rounds.

## E13 vs E14: what changed for postdocs

Since the 2025/2026 bargaining round, several states have moved **postdoctoral** positions into a new **E14** group (or are phasing it in), separating postdocs from doctoral researchers in the pay tables. Two practical points:

- **PhD candidates mostly remain on E13.** The reform targets the postdoc tier; if you're being hired for a doctorate, expect E13.
- **Check which state your position sits in.** TV-L states and non-TV-L states implement changes on different schedules, so a postdoc offer in Bavaria and one in Berlin may sit in different groups for the same starting date.

Treat any salary quote older than a year with suspicion — ask HR to confirm the current table value in writing.

## Reading your contract: the checklist

Before signing a German PhD contract, find and verify:

- **Pay group and percentage** — e.g. "TV-L E13, 65 %". If the percentage isn't stated, ask.
- **Duration and extension structure** — many contracts run 12–18 months at a time, renewed based on progress.
- **What the percentage means in practice:** a 50% contract usually still expects near-full-time presence for thesis progress; your research time is what remains after teaching and project duties.
- **TV-U vs TV-L:** universities under separate agreements (e.g. TV-U Berlin) have slightly different tables.
- **Familienerhöhung:** pay steps include small increases for children — check the family supplement if relevant.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does a 50% E13 PhD position pay?**
Roughly €2,300–€2,400 gross per month at the starting step (2026–27, varies by state), netting around €1,700–€1,900 depending on tax class. Always verify the current official TV-L table for your state before budgeting.

**Why is my PhD contract only 50% when I work full days?**
This is standard practice: the contract funds half your working time from a project; the rest is nominally your own research time. In reality the boundary blurs. Clarify expectations with the supervisor before accepting, and check the working-time clause in the contract.

**Do PhD researchers in Germany now get E14?**
No — E14 is being introduced for postdocs in some states from the 2025/2026 round. Doctoral researchers remain on E13 in nearly all cases. Confirm your specific pay group in the contract offer.

**Does the E13 salary increase during the PhD?**
Yes, through automatic pay steps based on years of experience, and through periodic collective wage increases negotiated by the unions. Step progression applies even within a single contract.

## Official source

TV-L tables change with every bargaining round and differ by state. Always confirm current amounts on the official **TV-L entgelttabellen** (oeffentlicher-dienst.info aggregates them, but check your university's HR page as the authoritative source) before making financial decisions.
