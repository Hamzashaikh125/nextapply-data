**Direct answer box (first 50 words):**

To study in the UK on a Student Route visa in 2027 you need a course offer from a licensed sponsor, proof of funds — £1,483 per month in London or £1,136 outside London for up to 9 months, held for 28 days — the ~£524 application fee, and possibly ATAS clearance. Doctoral graduates can stay 3 more years on the Graduate Route.

---

## What the UK Student Route asks of you

The Student Route (the current name for the Tier 4 visa) is a points-based visa. You score points for three things: a confirmed place, sufficient money, and English language ability that your university has assessed. Everything below flows from those three checks. Before you even start the visa, though, you need the offer and the funding — our guide to [how to apply for a Master's or PhD in the UK](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/how-to-apply-masters-phd-uk-official-links) covers that stage, and the [12-month application timeline](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/masters-phd-application-timeline-12-months) helps you sequence it.

## Financial requirements: the numbers that matter most

Money is where most rejections happen, so get this exactly right. You must show the money has been in your account (yours or your parents'/partner's) for **28 consecutive days**, with the final balance no older than **31 days** before your application.

| Requirement | London | Outside London |
|---|---|---|
| Maintenance per month (2026–27 rate, verify before applying) | £1,483 | £1,136 |
| Maximum months you must show | 9 | 9 |
| Total for a 9-month course | £13,347 | £10,224 |
| Visa application fee (main applicant) | ~£524 | ~£524 |
| Immigration Health Surcharge | Paid separately per year — check the current rate on gov.uk | Same |

Key rules students trip over:

- **Course fees first.** If you have not already paid your tuition deposit, add the first year's fees to the maintenance amount.
- **Currency conversion.** If your funds are in another currency, the exchange rate on the date of your application is what counts — leave a buffer.
- **28-day rule.** One dip below the required balance breaks the period. Do not touch the account while the 28 days build.
- **Acceptable funds.** Cash deposits in the bank, a loan letter, or official financial sponsorship. Fixed deposits, shares and crypto do not count.
- **Scholarship holders.** A fully funded scholarship can cover the maintenance evidence — bring the award letter. See [fully funded scholarships](https://nextapply.co.uk/fully-funded-scholarships) for options that meet the bar, and check whether awards like the [Chevening Scholarship](https://nextapply.co.uk/scholarship-chevening-uk-government) list what they cover.

## ATAS: do you need it?

The Academic Technology Approval Scheme applies to certain research-sensitive subjects — largely science, engineering and technology fields — for research Master's and PhD students. If your subject appears on the ATAS list, you need a certificate **before** you apply for the visa.

Practical points:

- Apply for ATAS through the gov.uk portal using your offer letter's CAH3 code — the code, not the course title, decides whether ATAS applies.
- Certificates are usually issued within weeks, but can take longer; apply as soon as you hold an offer.
- A certificate is valid for one visa application. If your course changes, you may need a new one.

## English language and CAS

Your university issues the **CAS (Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies)** — a digital reference number — once you accept an unconditional offer. The CAS is the anchor of the whole application: financial evidence is assessed against its start date, and English is usually proved through your university's own assessment (IELTS, TOEFL, PTE or a degree taught in English). Score requirements differ by university; our [IELTS vs TOEFL vs PTE comparison for UK PhDs](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/ielts-vs-toefl-vs-pte-uk-phd-2027) breaks down typical thresholds.

You cannot apply for the visa more than **6 months before** your course start date, and decisions typically take around 3 weeks, longer during peak season.

## Work rights during your course

| Situation | Work allowed |
|---|---|
| Full-time PhD student | Up to 20 hours/week in term time, full-time in vacations |
| Full-time taught Master's student | Up to 20 hours/week in term time |
| Placement or year abroad element | As permitted on your CAS |
| Dependants of PhD students | Generally may work — check current rules |

The 20-hour limit is measured per week and includes unpaid work. Exceeding it is a compliance breach, not a technicality. ## The Graduate Route: what happens after graduation

The Graduate Route lets you stay and work — or look for work — in the UK without sponsorship:

- **2 years** for Bachelor's and Master's graduates
- **3 years** for PhD graduates

You must be in the UK, have completed your course, and hold a valid Student visa when you apply. There is no job offer requirement and no minimum salary. That freedom is exactly what makes UK doctoral study attractive: three years of unrestricted work rights to convert your research into a career. ## Frequently asked questions

**How much money do I need for a UK student visa in 2027?**
For a 9-month course: £13,347 if studying in London, £10,224 outside London (maintenance only), plus any unpaid first-year tuition fee, held for 28 consecutive days. These are 2026–27 rates — check gov.uk for the rate in force when you apply.

**Do PhD students need ATAS?**
Often yes, depending on the research subject's CAH3 code. Most STEM doctorates require ATAS; most humanities and business subjects do not. Your offer letter will tell you — if it lists an ATAS requirement, apply for the certificate before the visa.

**Can I bring my family?**
Generally, only taught or research postgraduate students on courses of 9 months or longer can bring dependants, and current rules restrict which students qualify. Doctoral students are normally eligible. Verify dependant rules and fees before planning.

**How long does the UK student visa take?**
A standard decision usually takes about 3 weeks, though peak summer applications can take longer. Priority services exist for a fee. Apply as soon as the 6-month window before your course start opens.
