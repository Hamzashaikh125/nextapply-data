## What "tuition-free" actually means in 2027

Public universities in Germany do not charge tuition for the vast majority of Bachelor's and consecutive Master's programmes — for international students too. Three things are often confused with tuition:

1. **Semester contribution (Semesterbeitrag):** €150–€350 per semester, covering administration, student services and usually a regional transport ticket. Everyone pays it, including Germans.
2. **Non-consecutive Master's exceptions:** some states (notably Baden-Württemberg) charge non-EU students €1,500 per semester for certain programmes. Check your specific course.
3. **Private universities:** these do charge tuition (€10,000+ per year) and are a different market entirely.

The rule of thumb: **find the course's fee page, not a general article** — including this one. Fee rules are state-level and course-level, and they change.

## Can you realistically fund yourself? Four routes

### 1. Scholarships

The [DAAD](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/daad-scholarships-international-students-complete-guide) is Germany's flagship funder — €992/month for Master's students and around €1,400/month for doctoral candidates on its main schemes — but dozens of political foundations (Friedrich Ebert, Konrad Adenauer, Heinrich Böll and others) fund international students too. Research-focused students can also start with a funded route like the [Max Planck internship](https://nextapply.co.uk/max-planck-internship-2027-germany-fully-funded-research-program), which pays €1,300/month plus free housing for 12–14 weeks and often becomes a stepping stone into funded PhD positions.

### 2. Working alongside studies

International students may work **140 full days or 280 half days per year** under current student visa rules. A typical HiWi (research assistant) or minijob pays €13–€17/hour, which realistically covers a meaningful part of living costs. Warning: students whose German is weak usually find English-sufficient jobs in bigger cities only.

### 3. PhD employment

At doctoral level, "free study" is the wrong frame: most German PhDs are **paid jobs** (TV-L E13, 50–75%) rather than self-funded study. If you aim for a doctorate, you may need no personal funding at all — see [how German PhD pay works](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/phd-salary-germany-tv-l-e13-explained).

### 4. Family savings + part-time work

The classic route: blocked account for the visa, then part-time income plus careful budgeting in a small university town rather than Munich.

## Step-by-step: getting to a German university for 2027

1. **Choose programmes, not just cities.** Use the official DAAD database and check admission requirements per course — our [guide to finding the right DAAD scheme](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/daad-scholarships-international-students-complete-guide) covers scholarship matching.
2. **Check language requirements early.** English-taught Master's usually need IELTS ~6.5; German-taught programmes need certified German (often TestDaF 4 / DSH-2). Our [IELTS vs TOEFL vs PTE comparison](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/ielts-vs-toefl-vs-pte-uk-phd-2027) helps you pick a test accepted broadly.
3. **Prepare documents** — certified translations, APS certificate (for China, India, Vietnam), Uni-Assist evaluation where required. This stage alone takes 2–4 months; the [12-month application timeline](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/masters-phd-application-timeline-12-months) keeps it on schedule.
4. **Apply via Uni-Assist or the university portal** by the deadline — winter semester deadlines typically fall between January and July; summer intake is smaller.
5. **Secure funding evidence** for the visa (blocked account, scholarship letter, or sponsor declaration).
6. **Apply for the student visa** at the German embassy/consulate in your country — allow 6–12 weeks.
7. **Find housing before you fly** — this is genuinely the hardest step. Studierenwerk halls waitlists are long in big cities; temporary accommodation + in-person searching is a normal strategy.

## Is Germany still worth it compared to the UK?

For most self-funding students, yes: no tuition plus moderate living costs versus £20,000+ international tuition in the UK is a decisive gap. The trade-offs are longer degree structures, German-language daily life, and a housing market that punishes late starters. ## Frequently asked questions

**Is university really free in Germany for international students?**
At most public universities for most programmes, yes — you pay only the semester contribution. Exceptions: Baden-Württemberg's non-EU fee for some programmes and all private universities.

**How much money do I need in the bank for a German student visa?**
Around €11,904 (2026 blocked-account rate) for one year, adjusted annually. Alternatives include a scholarship award letter or a sponsor's declaration. Amounts change — verify with your embassy.

**Can I study in Germany without IELTS?**
Some universities accept a Medium of Instruction certificate or prior English-taught degrees, but requirements are course-specific and tightening. See our [guide to applying without IELTS](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/masters-in-europe-without-ielts).

**What is the cheapest city to study in Germany?**
Smaller university towns (e.g. in Saxony, Thuringia or parts of NRW outside Cologne/Düsseldorf) typically offer rents 30–40% below Munich or Hamburg with the same tuition-free courses.

## Official sources

Tuition rules, visa amounts and blocked-account figures come from German federal and state regulations and change annually. Confirm current figures on **daad.de**, your university's fee page and the German embassy before making financial decisions.
