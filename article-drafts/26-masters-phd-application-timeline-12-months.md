**Direct answer box (first 50 words):**

Work backwards from an autumn start: 12 months out, shortlist programmes and book language tests; months 10–8, draft your CV, proposal and personal statement; months 7–5, request references and contact supervisors; months 4–2, submit applications; the final two months cover interviews, offers and visa. Deadlines cluster between October and January.

---

## Why a 12-month runway is the realistic minimum

Most applicants underestimate two things: how early deadlines fall (many scholarship and UK PhD deadlines land in October–December for the following autumn) and how long documents take. A single research proposal needs four to six weeks of drafting; a referee who agrees "in principle" may need six weeks of chasing. Running the whole process on a 12-month timeline absorbs both without panic.

The table below assumes a **September/October 2027 start**. If your target country starts in January or February (common for some Australian and continental programmes), shift everything by four months.

## Three rules that keep the timeline alive

**1. References before holidays.** The single most common December bottleneck is a referee who agreed verbally but hasn't started. Give them the deadline, the project description and your CV in one package when you first ask — the [request templates](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/how-to-request-letter-of-recommendation-email-templates) show the exact email.

**2. Supervisor contact is a thread, not an event.** Expect one or two weeks of silence and one follow-up; our [follow-up guide](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/how-to-follow-up-phd-supervisor-does-not-reply) covers the polite nudge and when to move to a second supervisor.

**3. Parallel funding, parallel applications.** Never apply to one university and one scholarship in sequence. Scholarship cycles (for example the [DAAD scholarships for Germany](https://nextapply.co.uk/scholarship-daad-germany) or [fully funded listings on NextApply](https://nextapply.co.uk/fully-funded-scholarships)) close months before university portals — they need their own deadlines in your tracker.

## If you're starting late

Eight months out, the plan still works if you compress months 12–10 into two weeks: shortlist from pre-made rankings, book the nearest test date, and email referees the same day. Four months out, prioritise programmes with spring deadlines and rolling admissions, and lean on externally funded routes whose deadlines haven't passed. And if a decision doesn't come your way this cycle, our guide on [what to do after a PhD rejection](https://nextapply.co.uk/articles/phd-application-rejected-what-next) turns a missed cycle into next year's stronger application.

## Frequently asked questions

**When should I start preparing for autumn 2027 admission?**
October 2026 — twelve months ahead. Scholarship deadlines close earliest, and references, tests and proposals all eat more calendar time than applicants expect.

**Can I apply without IELTS?**
Some universities waive the test based on prior English-medium education or their own assessments; rules vary by country and university. Check before assuming, and don't leave the decision past month 9 — a failed waiver means a rushed test booking.

**How many programmes should I apply to?**
Six to ten is a sensible range for funded places: enough to survive rejections, few enough that every application stays tailored. Ten identical applications beat thirty lazy ones.

**What takes longer than people expect?**
References, official transcripts, language test results, and — for PhDs — supervisor responses. Everything on this timeline exists to keep those four items off your critical path.
